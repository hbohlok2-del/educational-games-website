---
title: Newton's Second Law
order: 1
summary: Relating force, mass, and acceleration
---

Newton's second law relates the net force on an object to its mass and acceleration:

$$\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = m\vec{a}$$

Force and acceleration are vectors — they point in the same direction, since mass is always positive. In one dimension it's usually written without the arrows:

$$F = ma$$

## Worked example

A $1200\ \text{kg}$ car accelerates from rest to $27\ \text{m/s}$ in $9\ \text{s}$ on a flat road. What net force does the engine need to produce?

First find the acceleration:

$$a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{27\ \text{m/s}}{9\ \text{s}} = 3\ \text{m/s}^2$$

Then apply the second law:

$$F = ma = (1200\ \text{kg})(3\ \text{m/s}^2) = 3600\ \text{N}$$

## Related quantity: momentum

Since $\vec{F} = m\vec{a} = m\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = \frac{d(m\vec{v})}{dt}$, the second law can also be written in terms of momentum $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$:

$$\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}$$
