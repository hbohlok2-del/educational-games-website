---
title: Balancing Chemical Equations
order: 1
summary: Conserving atoms on both sides of a reaction
---

A balanced chemical equation has the same number of atoms of each element on both sides. Combustion of methane, unbalanced:

$$\ce{CH4 + O2 -> CO2 + H2O}$$

Count atoms on each side: the left has 1 C, 4 H, 2 O; the right has 1 C, 2 H, 3 O. Hydrogen and oxygen don't match yet.

Balance hydrogen first by putting a 2 in front of $\ce{H2O}$:

$$\ce{CH4 + O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O}$$

Now the right side has 1 C, 4 H, 4 O. Oxygen on the left is still short — 2 $\ce{O2}$ gives 4 O:

$$\ce{CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O}$$

Both sides now have 1 C, 4 H, 4 O — the equation is balanced.

## Isotope notation

The same `\ce{}` command handles isotopes, as in this decay of carbon-14:

$$\ce{^{14}\_6C -> ^{14}\_7N + e-}$$
