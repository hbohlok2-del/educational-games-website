A **composite figure** is made of simpler shapes. To find its area, decompose it into shapes you know, find the area of each one, and add.

## The method

1. **Decompose** the figure into rectangles, triangles and other familiar shapes.
2. **Find each area**: rectangle $A = bh$, triangle $A = \tfrac12 bh$, trapezoid $A = \tfrac12 h(b_1 + b_2)$.
3. **Add** the areas. Check that every length uses the same unit first.

## Worked example: a trapezoid

A trapezoid is $26$ in. across the top, $60$ in. along the bottom and $38$ in. tall. Split it into a rectangle and two right triangles. Each triangle has base $\frac{60 - 26}{2} = 17$ in.

$$26(38) + 2\left[\tfrac12(17)(38)\right] = 988 + 646 = 1{,}634 \text{ in}^2$$

The trapezoid formula gives the same answer: $\tfrac12(26 + 60)(38) = 1{,}634$ in².

## Surface area

The **surface area** of a solid is the total area of all its faces. Unfold the solid into a **net** so you can see every face, then add their areas. A triangular prism has five faces: two triangles and three rectangles. Count them before you add, so no face is missed.
