title: Solving Quadratic Equations
order: 1
summary: Completing the square and the quadratic formula
---

A quadratic equation has the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, with $a \neq 0$. Its two solutions are given by the quadratic formula:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

The quantity under the square root, $b^2 - 4ac$, is called the **discriminant**. It tells you what kind of roots to expect without solving the equation:

- $b^2 - 4ac > 0$ — two distinct real roots
- $b^2 - 4ac = 0$ — one repeated real root
- $b^2 - 4ac < 0$ — a complex-conjugate pair

## Worked example

Solve $2x^2 - 4x - 6 = 0$.

Here $a = 2$, $b = -4$, $c = -6$, so the discriminant is $(-4)^2 - 4(2)(-6) = 16 + 48 = 64$.

$$x = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{64}}{4} = \frac{4 \pm 8}{4}$$

So $x = 3$ or $x = -1$.
